06How we position it

Lead with sovereignty. Support with neutrality. Prove with speed.

In that order, in every meeting, in every market in this deck. The global platform vendors beat us on breadth and always will. We win on the one thing they cannot structurally offer.

LeadSovereign by construction

Your servers, your data, your AI model, with nothing crossing the boundary when the regulator requires it. The majors are cloud-native by design. Atlantic was built for the other case from the start.

SupportNo captive hardware, ever

We will never sell the chiller, the controller, the fire panel or the badge reader. Atlantic overlays whatever mixed estate a customer owns. Our own qualified sensors and tags are available to anyone who wants one vendor, and never required.

ProveFast enough to prove it

Weeks to a working pilot on real devices, not quarters to a design review. On one gate line, Atlantic Simulate found the real bottleneck was the gate mechanism, not the radio, before anyone bought a gate.

11Market
Now

Healthcare & Facilities: the beachhead, extended

The opportunity

Asset and equipment tracking, room and bed availability, occupancy: already sold. New: cashless payment (staff cafeterias, vending, campus retail) and hardened safety alerting, as extensions of the same asset layer.

The wedge

Four systems today (access control, cafeteria payment, asset tracking, lone-worker safety), bought at different times and never reconciled. “Infrared beams, not cameras” is a provable privacy difference from camera-based competitors, and it extends cleanly to payment.

How to attack it
  • Ask which two systems ops most often reconciles by hand, then demo that exact correlation
  • Start payment closed-loop (a campus stored-value wallet), not on open bank-card rails
  • Sell safety as alerting, never as interlock
  • Land with one pillar already in place and expand through the existing QBR cadence
WatchProximity alerting must never drift into stopping equipment automatically. That is safety-instrumented control, a permanent non-goal.
12Market
Now

Aviation, Transit & Civic: passenger flow and reliability

The opportunity

Passenger flow, wayfinding, terminal reliability and crowding, visible before the bottleneck forms. Terminals, kiosks and sensors across many sites, monitored from one console with one data model.

The wedge

A multi-site network by nature, with a named ops owner already in place (terminal or station operations) and a real data-residency story for national operators. The natural on-ramp to transit payment.

How to attack it
  • Lead discovery with dwell-time and crowding pain, not the platform pitch
  • Use existing terminal and kiosk monitoring as the entry pillar
  • Cite the aviation use case when opening a ports or logistics conversation
  • Treat this as the proof point for the transit-payment pitch, not a separate motion
WatchDon't let this stay a monitoring-only account. It is the natural door into the payment opportunity next door.

Transit: UWB payment, location, and the network around both

The window · JCB, Resona and Odawara hands-free UWB bus payment
18–36 month window
FY2026Technical trials
2027Small rollouts
2028Full deployment
The opportunity

An 18–36 month land grab, led by payment companies rather than operations companies. The timeline above is the window.

The wedge

Fare-linked movement data (who rode, when, where, tied to a payment identity, at national scale) is among the most sensitive data a government holds. For many transit ministries, routing it through a foreign-hosted payments cloud is a policy non-starter. We already run thousands of payment devices in retail and transit across Singapore, Japan, Thailand and the Philippines.

How to attack it
  • Sell the tap as one more event on the correlated timeline, not a fintech feature
  • Secure a payment-settlement or scheme partner now: we verify the tap, a partner settles it
  • Open with national or city transit authorities that have an explicit residency mandate
  • Use existing aviation and civic references to open the conversation
WatchWe are not a payment scheme and should not try to become one. A deployed device base is not settlement or PCI scope, which sit outside what STYL does today. Partner, don't build alone.

Next

Government & Defense estates

The opportunity

Secure inventory, personnel and visitor movement, and access control across a multi-site government estate. These are the tracking, safety and asset pillars we already sell, aimed at a buyer for whom data residency is a legal mandate.

The wedge

Arguably a cleaner fit than transit: no payment-certification burden, no new safety liability, and the purest form of the sovereignty argument. The right place to sharpen the pitch before harder, higher-stakes conversations.

How to attack it
  • Open with the architecture workshop, since this buyer expects a residency conversation first
  • Lead entirely with sovereignty; neutrality and speed are supporting points
  • Use it as an internal proving ground for the sovereignty pitch before the transit push
  • Plan the pilot timeline around a longer security accreditation cycle
WatchProcurement runs longer here than a facilities deal. Resource the pursuit for a long sales cycle, not a six-week pilot.

Next

Ports, Maritime & Airport Cargo

The opportunity

Container and asset tracking, customs zones and safety areas across a multi-vendor, multi-operator estate. The freight-side extension of the aviation and transit segment we already hold.

The wedge

The classic overlay pitch, with our own aviation work as the reference to open with. The cheapest expansion on this list: same buyer relationships, same reference stories, new asset class.

How to attack it
  • Open through existing aviation and transit relationships and references
  • Position it as the freight side of a segment we already serve, not a new vertical
  • Emphasise the mixed-vendor overlay; ports run famously fragmented estates
  • Target multi-terminal operators before single-terminal ones, for the expansion story
WatchCustoms and safety-zone requirements vary by jurisdiction. Confirm compliance scope in the workshop, before the pilot is scoped.
16Market
Later

Manufacturing, Warehousing & Industrial Safety

The opportunity

The fastest-growing RTLS vertical, with UWB already preferred in large open spaces, which suits the spatial twin. Separately, mining, oil & gas and construction have real regulatory tailwinds for UWB worker-proximity warning.

The wedge

A weaker sovereignty story: a private operator feels less regulatory pressure than a hospital or a ministry. It sells better on correlation and speed-to-pilot. Industrial safety also needs a new buyer: an HSE officer, not an operations lead.

How to attack it
  • Sequence this after a reference deployment exists elsewhere
  • Lead with speed-to-pilot and correlation, not sovereignty
  • For industrial safety, sell alerting only, never interlock, exactly as in facilities
  • Use STYL's ruggedised environmental-sensing history since 2008 as credibility
WatchThe HSE or safety officer is a new buyer persona. Don't assume the facilities playbook transfers to this relationship untested.

Hold

Stadiums, events and retail: real markets, wrong shape today

Stadiums, events & venues

A real UWB/RTLS payment market, but RFID-dominated today. A single venue fails our own qualification criteria: one site, no expansion story, and a buyer (venue ops or marketing) who isn't the daily-pain operations owner we sell to elsewhere.

Reconsider ifa multi-venue operator, such as a stadium group or arena management company, initiates.

Retail

Inventory and loss prevention are real RTLS use cases, but the field is crowded with BLE/RFID incumbents, single-store deployments rarely clear our device and site thresholds, and there is no residency story to lean on.

Reconsider ifa large, mixed-format, multi-site retailer comes forward. Same bar as everywhere else.

Don't chase the size of the category. Chase whether the counterparty clears the same bar every other market on this list has to clear.

20GTM channel · silicon partners
Channel

Atlantic Simulate: a wedge through every silicon partner's channel

The opportunity

Atlantic Simulate lets a silicon partner's own customer walk their site before any hardware ships. NXP is the first partner deck built on it: a live customer conversation, not just an evaluation kit.

The wedge

The model that sizes a deployment is the model that later runs it, so nothing is rebuilt between winning an evaluation and going live. On one rail-gate line it found the real bottleneck was the gate mechanism, not the radio, before anyone bought a gate.

How to attack it
  • Package a named-partner version, NXP RW612-class first, as a joint pilot asset
  • Route their evaluation-kit customers into our architecture workshop, not a lab demo
  • Reuse it in every vertical pitch as the proof mechanism, not a new product
  • Negotiate co-marketing and event presence into the partnership, not just an API deal
WatchWe are not a silicon vendor and take no chip-level commitment. Simulate showcases a partner's hardware; it never substitutes for it.

26Risk & discipline

A payment-settlement partner, secured early

PCI scope, scheme approval and settlement liability sit outside STYL today. Transit and facilities payment both need this partner in place before we commit dates to a customer.

Discipline

Payment and safety alerting are not live today. Carry the quarter in the sentence, exactly as the playbook already requires for firmware rollout and plain-language query.

Permanent boundary

The non-goal stays a non-goal

Safety-instrumented control, where a tag or badge actually stops equipment, stays out of scope in facilities and industrial safety alike.

Governance

Pricing needs Commercial and Finance sign-off
